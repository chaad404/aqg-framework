You are writing multiple-choice questions for a university state exam (BSc in Computer Science and Engineering). The candidates have studied the material in <context>. Write {NUM_QUESTIONS} questions based on it.

Every question must follow all of these rules.

THE QUESTION (STEM)
1. It tests one important concept that the context explains: a definition, a mechanism, a cause and its effect, a comparison, or a calculation. Do not ask about trivia such as names, dates, or page details.
2. It uses the context's own technical terms, so a student who studied the material recognises what is being asked.
3. It is a complete, self-contained question of one or two sentences, about 15 to 35 words long. It never mentions "the text", "the context", or "the passage".
4. It asks about something different from every other question in this set.

THE CORRECT ANSWER
5. It is unambiguously correct according to the context. In "evidence", copy word for word the sentence or sentences from the context that prove it (at most 300 characters).

THE THREE DISTRACTORS (WRONG OPTIONS)
6. Each one is a mistake that a student who only half knows this topic would make. Examples: a closely related concept from the same context, a swapped cause and effect or order, a wrong condition or limit, or a plausible but incorrect value.
7. Each one is clearly wrong according to the context. It must not be a paraphrase, a partial version, or a special case of the correct answer, and it must not also be true.
8. The three distractors make three different mistakes. They must not be rewordings of each other.

ALL FOUR OPTIONS
9. They share the same grammatical form and have similar lengths (within about 20% of each other). The correct answer must not be the longest or the most detailed option.
10. Do not use "all of the above" or "none of the above". Do not use absolute words such as "always" or "never" only in the wrong options.

Spread the correct answers over the positions A, B, C and D.

Return one JSON object and nothing else, in this format:
